Fraud Risk Scoring

A model that estimates how likely a card payment is to be fraud, trained on six months of e-commerce transactions. This page shows what its scores are worth in money, how it compares with simpler models, and why it flags a transaction.

What the scores are worth

Every transaction gets a calibrated fraud probability. The rule then picks the action with the lowest expected loss: approve it, ask the customer to confirm (for example with an SMS code), or decline it. About

Time-based split

Models learn on the first four months, choices are made on the fifth, and the sixth is touched once, at the end. A random split would let the model learn from the future and overstate its quality.

Method

  • Data: IEEE-CIS Fraud Detection (Vesta), 590,540 card payments over 182 days, 3.5% fraud.
  • Models: logistic regression, random forest, CatBoost and LightGBM tuned with Optuna. LightGBM uses 440 columns, nine of them built in this project: the day a card and its D-column counters started.
  • Probabilities are calibrated with Platt scaling on the validation month.
  • Default costs: a missed fraud costs its amount plus a $20 chargeback fee, declining an honest customer $10, a confirmation $2 per honest customer, passed by 10% of fraudsters.
  • Explanations: SHAP values of LightGBM on 5,000 validation transactions.

Limitations

  • The costs are assumptions, not Vesta's figures; the simulator above shows how the result moves with them.
  • Quality drops in the first weeks after the training data, then holds: PR-AUC is 0.654 on the validation month (optimistic, as it was used for tuning), 0.552 on its second half and 0.572 on the test month. Frequent retraining would keep the model in its strongest weeks.
  • A chargeback also marks the card's later payments as fraud, and the newest labels may not be final.
  • Most columns are masked, so explanations often name a column whose meaning is unknown.
  • One test month: other periods may give other numbers.

Data: IEEE-CIS Fraud Detection (Vesta Corporation, via Kaggle). The competition rules do not allow redistributing the data, so this page shows only aggregated results: totals, curves and model explanations.
